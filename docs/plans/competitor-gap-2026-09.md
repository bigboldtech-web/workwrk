# Competitor gap decision document, September 2026

Status: research only. Nothing in this document has been built, scheduled or promised. Every proposal is a yes/no for the founder.

Sources: three surveys run on Mobbin (ClickUp web, monday.com web) with every cited screen inspected as an image, plus a read-only pass over this repo (prisma/schema.prisma, src/lib, src/components/board-view, src/app/(dashboard)). Repo paths are relative to the repo root. Anything a competitor is believed to have but that no Mobbin screen shows is marked **unverified** and is not used to justify a recommendation.

Two constraints shaped every line: nothing that works today is proposed for removal, and no new UI surface is proposed without being called out as one, per the standing "stop inventing UI surfaces" mandate.

## 1. Honest summary

WorkwrK is genuinely competitive, and in places ahead, on breadth of primitives: seventeen rendered view types (ClickUp exposes eleven in its required-views admin, Monday ten in its Add View menu), a forty-type field catalogue, a real spreadsheet engine inside Tables that neither competitor has, a first-party whiteboard, a four-kind SOP system with acknowledgement and process runs that neither competitor has at all, Goals with typed key results linked to permanent KPIs, timesheets with a submit/approve/reject loop, an automation engine with nested AND/OR conditions and usage metering, and AI agents with tools and cron schedules. Monday's 2023 captures show no AI at all; WorkwrK has AI in docs, tables, forms, canvas, KRA and the inbox. Where it is genuinely behind is depth on the things a buyer tests in the first hour: there is no dependency picker on a task even though the link kinds exist in the schema, no workspace work schedule feeding capacity, no billable flag or estimate-versus-tracked view, no multi-List dashboard (the entity exists but the route is a 308 redirect and the rail entry was removed), the automation vocabulary has three task triggers and six actions and still carries Cashkr-era lead/quote/pickup/payment triggers, the notification model has four email categories and no task-level ones, the board permission model is a two-step Member/Guest split where Monday offers a four-rung editing ladder and ClickUp offers Limited Member plus custom roles, a task cannot be shared by public link (Docs and SOPs can), and the form builder exposes six question types where ClickUp exposes twelve with field mapping. None of these are architecturally hard; most are a column, a picker or a settings page on top of models already in the schema. The risk is not capability, it is polish and completeness on the surfaces a buyer compares side by side.

## 4. The top seven, argued

### 1. Multi-List dashboards and a Space Overview

Why it earns first place: it is the one thing on this list that a buyer evaluates before they log in, because it is on every competitor's marketing page and it is what the person with the budget actually looks at. ClickUp's [Add Card catalogue](https://mobbin.com/screens/36caf732-142f-4398-8824-4a46337d7490) and Monday's [Widgets Center](https://mobbin.com/screens/2628afd1-9513-4e24-b418-f9bc95916530) are near identical: counters, a status pie, a priority bar, an embedded list, a text block, a chart. WorkwrK already renders every one of those inside a List (board-chart-view, board-dashboard-view, pivot). What is missing is a canvas that points those same renderers at more than one List.

What it depends on: the Dashboard model (line 5780) already has widgets Json and spaceId. It needs a real page under a route that is not a redirect, a widget picker that reuses the existing chart and stat renderers, and a source picker over Spaces and Lists. The Space Overview is the same widget set with the source pinned to one Space, which is why the two are one decision. This is a reversal of the 2026-08-28 decision to remove Dashboards from the rail, so the founder must say yes explicitly; the recommendation is to bring it back under the Work hub as a Space tab rather than as a rail entry, so the eight-hub rail stays intact.

Slot: Phase 5 (data) for the widget canvas, since it is a reporting surface over tables and lists; the Space Overview tab belongs with spaces-boards and can trail into Phase 6 when people widgets (workload by person) are ready.

### 2. Automation completeness

Why: the engine is better than the vocabulary. `src/lib/automation/conditions.ts` evaluates nested AND/OR groups with fourteen operators including within_next_days and older_than, and `retry.ts`, `idempotency.ts` and `usage.ts` are the parts competitors get wrong. Yet `registry-triggers.ts` offers three task triggers and eleven Cashkr-era ones (lead.created, quote.accepted, pickup.scheduled, payment.failed), and `registry-actions.ts` offers six actions. Monday's whole [recipe library](https://mobbin.com/screens/20939958-e52c-4ae9-9289-864fcbfd8c72) runs on about eight triggers and ten actions; the ones WorkwrK lacks are date-arrives, every-time-period, field-changed, and the actions move-to-list, archive, add-comment, set-field. ClickUp's [condition and chained actions](https://mobbin.com/screens/e97dc953-7425-4e32-a143-42a77f55e25f) are a UI over a capability WorkwrK already has server-side.

What it depends on: nothing new in the schema. Trigger keys and action keys are registry entries; date-arrives needs a cron sweep, which the reminders cron already models. The condition step and "and then" chaining are editor work in `automation/workflows/[id]`. Hiding the non-PPMS triggers is a registry flag, not a deletion, so nothing breaks for any org that has them.

Slot: Phase 7 (AI, automation and add-ons). This is the spine of that phase.

### 3. Dependencies on the task and on the Gantt

Why: it is the cheapest high-value item on the list and it is table stakes. ClickUp's [Dependencies modal](https://mobbin.com/screens/fdb0e49d-6448-4875-8dba-3277bbabfa88) is three sections with a task search. The schema comment at `prisma/schema.prisma` line 6233 says the Related section renders "Waiting on" and "Blocking" groups once BLOCKS and WAITING_ON links exist, but `src/components/board-view/` contains zero references to either enum value, and `board-item-detail.tsx` treats Related as attachments. So there is no way for a user to create a dependency today. The Gantt (`board-gantt-view.tsx`) already has drag, resize and a critical-path reference at line 162, so arrows are a rendering change over the same links.

What it depends on: EntityLink rows with relationKind BLOCKS/WAITING_ON, which exist. One picker component (the existing `link-existing-picker.tsx` is the base), one section in task detail, one overlay on Gantt. A "blocked" badge in list rows is optional and small.

Slot: Phase 4 (time) for the Gantt arrows and the planner effect; the task-side picker is a Phase 2 follow-up that should land at the same time so the arrows have something to draw.

### 4. Work schedule and capacity

Why: WorkwrK's positioning is Workday for SMB, and Workday's defining primitive is the working calendar. ClickUp's [Work Schedule](https://mobbin.com/screens/9a17c133-0a08-4f2e-b740-71aed5b8b737) sets workweek days, hours and holidays, derives daily and weekly capacity, and feeds [Workload](https://mobbin.com/screens/95dd15f8-941f-4898-aee0-3ccceafccee9) and [Team capacity](https://mobbin.com/screens/7db81793-687b-44e3-9eb0-102ec1a53443). WorkwrK's `workload-grid.tsx` uses a hoursPerDay constant and there are zero hits for a work-schedule model. Without it the Workload view, the timesheet totals and any future leave or overtime story are built on a hard-coded number.

What it depends on: a small org-level settings record (days, hours, holidays) and an optional per-person override, then two consumers: workload-grid.tsx and timesheet-manager.tsx. Everything else on the people side (leave, clock, reviews) reads from it later.

Slot: Phase 4 (time and talk) for the settings and the two consumers; Phase 6 (people) consumes it for per-person overrides in the directory.

### 5. Task-level notification model

Why: the memory notes from the system-alignment audit recorded "zero task notifications" and a "dead bell popover" as Wave-1 truths. The inbox has since been rebuilt (Primary/Other/Mentions/Snoozed/Cleared), but `EmailPreference` at line 2145 still only knows kra, review, sop, kudos and a daily digest. ClickUp's [presets](https://mobbin.com/screens/7e0bfd6c-d380-4ce9-ad4a-6261d14a6667) and Monday's [email checkboxes](https://mobbin.com/screens/866f1001-7896-45b1-b4c1-7da5176a2050) both come down to the same six events: assigned to me, mentioned, status changed on a task I follow, comment on a task I follow, due soon, overdue. Monday's [per-board mute](https://mobbin.com/screens/710c3cf4-2a76-467c-9003-2beeb7b8e6d6) is the same notifyLevel WorkwrK already has on ConversationMember, moved onto BoardMember.

What it depends on: the watchers concept (present in task-detail-body.tsx), the Notification model (present), and an additive set of preference keys. Presets (Default / Focused / Custom) are a UI convenience over the keys. Per-List mute is one column on BoardMember.

Slot: Phase 8 (settings and my settings) for the preference page; the emit points are Phase 2 territory and should be audited as part of this item.

### 6. Board permission ladder and public task link

Why: the current model is Member = edit, Guest = view, with the contribute/edit split already separating content from management in code (canContributeBoard vs canEditBoard). Monday's [ladder](https://mobbin.com/screens/f430ef3a-8b23-4e82-9c6f-f6daa73e5c2f) adds the two rungs mid-market buyers actually ask for: "edit only rows assigned to them" and "comment only". ClickUp's [Share task](https://mobbin.com/screens/25c1b2aa-6bbf-4ced-8562-f53a6ae25d8c) with a public view-only link is how a client or vendor sees one task without a seat; WorkwrK already has that mechanism for Docs and SOPs (`src/app/(public)/share/{doc,sop}/[token]`) and not for tasks.

What it depends on: the access engine planned for Phase 0 (the Simple ladder decision). Adding two rungs to the board membership role is additive; the public task page reuses the share-token pattern. The "assigned only" rung already has a precedent in the me/items unfiltered-for-owned rule.

Slot: Phase 8 (settings and access). Do not build it ahead of the access engine or it will be built twice.

### 7. Form builder parity

Why: forms are the fastest route to a second team using the product. ClickUp's [question types](https://mobbin.com/screens/d2a256d8-de56-4a54-9183-e36d3495470b) map each question to a new or existing field; Monday's [WorkForms](https://mobbin.com/screens/291244a5-987a-49c8-ba4f-767688b7629d) do the same. WorkwrK's FormDefinition already has isPublic, targetBoardId, targetTableId and fieldMappings, and the "Form Response" task type is seeded. The builder exposes six question types. Adding dropdown, multi-select, file upload, people, rating and a section block, plus a "map to existing field" picker and a required toggle, closes the visible gap without any model change.

What it depends on: the field catalogue (done) and the S3 presign path for uploads (done). Templates (Feedback, Intake, IT request) are Template Center rows.

Slot: Phase 5 (data: tables, forms, embeds, import).

Near misses, in order: undo toasts everywhere (gap 9, small, should ride along with whichever phase touches the bulk bar first); AI project update and StandUp (gap 12, Phase 7, once agents can write into a task or channel); tasks in multiple Lists (gap 10, wait for demand); time tracking depth (gap 5, Phase 4, pairs naturally with item 4 and should be scoped together if the founder says yes to both).

## 6. Questions for the founder

1. **Dashboards.** You removed Dashboards from the rail on 2026-08-28. Item 1 asks to bring the entity back as a Space tab and a standalone page under Work, reusing the chart and stat renderers that already exist. Yes, no, or only the Space Overview tab?
2. **Cashkr residue in automations.** `registry-triggers.ts` still offers lead, quote, pickup and payment triggers to every org. Hide them behind a product flag, keep them visible, or leave untouched until Phase 7?
3. **Permission ladder rungs.** Which of the two extra rungs matter for your buyers: "edit only rows assigned to me" or "comment only", or both? This sets the shape of the Phase 0 access engine's board rules and should be decided before that engine ships.
4. **Public task links.** Docs and SOPs can be shared by token. Should a task be shareable the same way (view-only, optional expiry), given it exposes assignee names and comments to someone without a seat?
5. **Work schedule scope.** One org-wide schedule (ClickUp's default) or per-person schedules from day one? The first is a week; the second touches the directory and belongs in Phase 6.
6. **Billable time.** Is time billing in scope for a PPMS, or is the timesheet strictly an internal attendance and capacity record? This decides whether item 5 in the gaps table is built at all.
7. **Tasks in multiple Lists.** ClickUp's Move-vs-Add model needs a join table and touches every visibility query. Wait for a customer to ask, or plan it now as part of the Phase 0 data layer while the tables are being pivoted anyway?
8. **Sprint tooling.** Confirm sprint points and burndown stay out. The Sprint model exists and someone will be tempted.
9. **AI placement.** ClickUp puts AI inline (task strip, selected-text Improve, field autofill, slash menu, search row). Sidekick is a hub. Should Phase 7 spend on inline entry points in task detail and the list, or on the agent runtime writing project updates and standups into channels? Both are valid; they are different weeks.
10. **Screenshot rule.** Items 1, 3 and 6 all put something visible on the task or the Space. Per the parity mandate, which competitor screen is the reference for each: the ClickUp one cited, the Monday one, or a fresh capture you will supply?
