# Time tracking and finding things

Two unglamorous surfaces you will use every day. Both have sharp edges worth knowing before you rely on them.

---

## Tracking time on a task

Time is tracked **against a task**. There are three ways to start:

- the **Track time** field in the task detail: a play button and your running total
- **Start timer** in a task row's `…` or right-click menu
- the topbar timer pill, which appears whenever a timer is running and stops it in one click

Only one timer runs at a time per person. While one is running, the pill in the topbar shows the task and a live clock; the task's own Track time field shows the same clock in red.

Click the total (not the play button) in the task detail to open the full tracker: your recent sessions, and a manual-entry form for time you forgot to track.

### The one gotcha with manual entries

**Stopping a running timer** on a task writes an entry into your weekly timesheet. **Adding a manual entry** does not. Manual entries count toward the task's tracked total and show in the session list, but they never reach your timesheet.

If the time needs to appear on your timesheet, run the timer.

---

## How tracked time becomes a timesheet

When you stop a task timer, the system:

1. Closes the session and stores its duration.
2. Finds (or creates) your **DRAFT** timesheet for the week that contains the stop time. Weeks start Monday, in UTC.
3. Appends a time entry to it, in hours rounded to two decimals, on that day, described with the task's title and linked back to the task.

If the week's timesheet has already been submitted or approved, **nothing is written**. A locked week is never silently modified. Track the time, then talk to whoever approves your sheets.

## The Timesheets page

`/timesheets` opens on four stat tiles (drafts, submitted, approved, hours logged) and four scopes:

| Scope | Who can use it | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mine** | Everyone | Your recent weeks. |
| **Approve queue** | Managers | Submitted weeks assigned to you, plus unassigned ones. |
| **Team** | Managers | Your full report tree, direct and indirect. |
| **All** | Managers | Org-wide. |

**Start this week** opens (or reopens) the current week's timesheet so entries have somewhere to land. It is idempotent: pressing it twice does not create two sheets.

Click a row to expand it and see its entries, with the task each entry came from.

Row actions depend on status:

- A **Draft** week can be **Submit**ted.
- A **Submitted** week can be **Retract**ed back to draft.
- In the **Approve queue**, a submitted week can be **Approve**d or **Reject**ed.

There is no control on this page for typing a time entry by hand. Entries arrive from stopped task timers.

## Finding things

### Search (Cmd/Ctrl + K)

The command palette opens with **Cmd + K** (**Ctrl + K** on Windows and Linux) or the search box in the topbar. Type at least two characters and results come back live from the server.

**What search actually covers today:**

- People
- Notes and documents (access-checked, so private notes never appear in someone else's results)
- SOPs
- Goals
- Departments
- Meetings
- Policies
- Announcements
- Ideas
- Legacy tasks (the older task table, not board tasks)

The query also still runs against four tables left over from removed finance modules: vendors, purchase orders, GL accounts and budget plans. If your database has rows in those, they will appear in results and their links go to `/procurement`, `/financials` and `/planning`, none of which exist any more. Those hits are dead ends.

**What search does not cover today:**

- **Tasks on Lists.** This is the big one. Board tasks are not in the search index, so searching a task title finds nothing. Use **Everything** or the List's own search box instead.
- Spaces, Folders and Lists
- Whiteboards, files and tables

Three more things to know about the palette, so you do not mistake decoration for data:

- With an **empty query**, the sections show **placeholder sample content** (invented tasks, documents, people and Spaces) as a layout demonstration. Several of those rows link to routes that do not exist. Ignore them and type your query.
- The **Actions** rows in the palette (Create task, New doc, Schedule meeting) are decorative. They carry neither a link nor a handler, so clicking one just closes the palette.
- The **Command** rows are a mixed bag. Most are real links (Open Planner, Open My Work, Go to Inbox, Go to Docs home, Go to Goals, Go to Timesheets, Create new Doc, Create new Task, Connect apps). Two point at routes that do not exist: **Go to Pulse** and **Create new Notepad**. Seven have no link and no handler at all, so they do nothing: **Show Favorites**, **Show Tray**, **Show Recent Activity**, **Set status**, **Update Slack status**, **Download Diagnostics** and **Feature Trace**.

The **Navigate** rows are all real links to real pages.

### Other keyboard shortcuts

| Shortcut | Does |
| --- | --- |
| **Cmd/Ctrl + K** | Open or close the search palette. |
| **Cmd/Ctrl + J** | Open or close the AI panel. |
| **Cmd/Ctrl + T** | Quick task modal. Some browsers reserve this for a new tab. |
| **Cmd/Ctrl + B** | Collapse or expand the sidebar. |
| **Cmd/Ctrl + 1-9** | Jump to the Nth pinned app in the rail. |
| **Escape** | Close the palette. |

### Better ways to find a task

Since search does not index board tasks, use these instead:

- **Everything** (`/everything`): every task you are allowed to read across all Spaces, newest 500, grouped by status, with the full List-view toolbar. The search box and filters in that toolbar work over the whole set.
- **A List's own search**: the magnifier in the board toolbar filters that List's tasks by title as you type.
- **My Wrk** (`/tasks`) and its sub-pages: **Assigned to me**, **Today & Overdue**, **Personal List**.
- **Filters** on any view, which are far more precise than text search: field, operator, value, joined with AND or OR, and savable.

### Finding notes and files

- The **Library** (`/library`) has a search box per tab and Space chips to narrow by Space.
- The **Docs** home has its own search, sort and tabs.
- **Mentions** (`/me/mentions`) collects every place across documents and SOPs where someone `@`-mentioned you, with the surrounding text as the excerpt. Clicking a row jumps to the exact block.

## Inbox, and what actually reaches it

`/inbox` sorts notifications into Primary, Other, Later (snoozed) and Cleared.

Be realistic about what arrives there. **Comment mentions on a task create a notification.** Assigning a task, changing its status, and a due date arriving currently create **none**. Until that changes, treat the Inbox as a mentions feed, not a work queue, and use My Wrk and the "assigned to me" filters to see what is on your plate.

Two related surfaces to set expectations on:

- **Assigned Comments** in the sidebar renders its empty state permanently. Assigning a comment to a person is not built yet, so there is no data behind either tab.
- **Favorites** in the sidebar (the section with your starred Spaces, Lists, Docs, Folders, tables, whiteboards and files) is the real favorites list. The separate `/favorites` page is something else entirely: it is the pinboard for your pinned AI chats.
