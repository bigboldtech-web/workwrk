# What is WorkwrK

WorkwrK is a **People + Project Management System (PPMS)**. It holds two things that are normally bought separately, and it holds them in one database so they can point at each other:

1. **A work management platform.** Spaces, Folders, Lists, tasks, 17 view types, custom fields, subtasks, comments, time tracking. This is the ClickUp/Monday half.
2. **A people and performance layer.** Job titles (Roles), the result areas each title owns (KRAs) with weights, the permanent gauges under those areas (KPIs), goals that hang off a single accountable person, and the reporting line that decides who sees what.

You use it to run the work, and to answer "who owns this, and how do we know it is going well" without leaving the product.

## Who it is for

WorkwrK is built for the company that actually does the work, not for an agency running it on someone else's behalf. Today the first audience is internal: our own team. Everything in this documentation is written for someone who has an account and is looking at the real screens.

Access is shaped by a fixed ladder of levels: Employee, Agent, Team Lead, Manager, Director, VP, C-Level, HR, plus Company Admin and Super Admin, which are granted by hand rather than picked from a dropdown. That ladder drives three doors:

- **Employees and Agents** see their own work and their own numbers.
- **Team Leads and Managers** see themselves plus their whole recursive report tree.
- **Directors, VPs, C-Level, HR, Company Admin and Super Admin** see the entire organization's alignment data.

This is enforced on the server in every alignment API, not by hiding nav links. An employee who types a manager URL lands on their own career home at `/people/me` instead.

## Why the two halves are one system

Most teams end up with a task tool that knows nothing about accountability and a performance tool that knows nothing about the work. The result is two truths: the board says the sprint is fine, the review says the person is not.

WorkwrK connects them at three specific joints that exist in the product today:

- **A task can be tagged to a KRA or a KPI.** The create-task modal and the task detail panel both carry an alignment control that stamps the KRA or KPI onto the task, so a piece of work carries the result area it serves.
- **A goal's Key Result can be measured by a KPI.** When a Key Result is linked to a KPI, its number is derived at read time from the KPI's latest reading, and hand check-ins on it are refused with an explanation rather than silently accepted. (The link is supported by the data model and the API; see the honest note below about the missing picker.)
- **A person's KRAs come from their job title.** Assigning someone to a Role seeds their KRA assignments from that Role's templates, at the Role's weights.

## What WorkwrK is not

**It is not an HRIS.** There is no payroll module, no benefits enrollment, no leave or attendance tracking, no recruiting or applicant tracking in the product. Those surfaces were removed. If you are looking for a place to run a pay cycle or approve time off, it is not here.

**It is not a payroll system.** WorkwrK does not calculate, file, or pay anything, and there is no pay run anywhere in the product.

**It is not a compliance system of record for employment.** The person profile carries job title, function, reporting manager, access level and status, because the alignment model needs them. It does not carry an employment file.

## What you get on day one

- **Work**: Spaces containing Folders containing Lists, and tasks inside those Lists, viewable as a table, a Kanban board, a calendar, a Gantt chart, a timeline, a workload grid, and more.
- **People**: a directory, an org chart built from reporting lines, a job-title library, and a personal career home for every user.
- **Alignment**: KRAs and KPIs managed per job title, monthly KPI recording, and goals with a single owner and many contributors.

## Honest notes

Documentation that overstates a product is worse than documentation that is missing. These are the gaps you will hit in the areas this page describes:

- **Linking a Key Result to a KPI has no picker.** The API accepts `kpiId` on a Key Result, the goal detail page renders "measured by KPI · <name>" for linked targets, and the check-in route correctly refuses hand entry on them. But there is no control in the UI that creates the link today. It can only be set through the API.
- **Departments are create-and-view only in the UI.** You can create a department and see the tree; renaming, assigning a head, re-parenting and deleting exist as API endpoints but have no controls on the page yet.
- **The invite modal does not collect department, role, or manager.** It collects emails, an access level, and the required KRA and SOP selections. You place the person into their function, job title and reporting line afterwards, from their profile.
- **KRA weights are not forced to total 100.** Each individual weight is validated to sit between 0 and 100, and the role page shows a running "weight N% of 100" that turns red above 100, but nothing blocks a save at 80% or 130%.
